# BlobForge

BlobForge is a custom 2D engine project for physical, destructible blob matter. The current executable is the first **Matter Lab**: a focused production-physics slice for filled tissue, grabbing, damage, splitting, stable rest, and diagnostics. The temporary WinForms renderer remains a test harness while the SDL/GPU renderer is developed.

The production direction is now designed from gameplay requirements rather than inherited Unity implementation notes:

- [Core game requirements](docs/CORE_GAME_REQUIREMENTS.md)
- [Production engine architecture](docs/ENGINE_ARCHITECTURE.md)

## Run

```powershell
dotnet run --project .\BlobForge\BlobForge.csproj
```

Controls:

- `I` while debug mode is enabled: open or close the developer test-arsenal menu. Normal play gets only the single weapon delivered by the centered dumbwaiter; the debug menu remains available for unrestricted weapon testing
- `T` or the visible `SPAWN TOKEN` developer button: spawn the one allowed physical reroll coin above the conveyor for pickup, conveyor, slot, and button testing
- Hover the butcher's cleaver and press `E` to equip it to the cursor; press `E` again to physically release it. Equipped tools remain held without a mouse button, inherit their actual simulated grip velocity when thrown, and expose a tool-specific primary action
- Hold right mouse and drag while most tools are equipped to set their resting/base rotation; hold `A` or `D` for smooth continuous counterclockwise/clockwise rotation of the same base angle. A cyan circle and amber direction line show the live angle. Rotation cancels any active attack/throw and blocks firing or swinging until RMB is released, but remains available during melee recovery. Guns and barrel tools initially follow their left-facing rack pose; other tools begin blade-up. The sledge is the exception: RMB toggles its floor slam between the left and right side instead of freely rotating it. The held slingshot is locked north and the boxing glove toggles only between left/right. For the lightsaber, the first left click ignites, later left clicks use the cleaver-relative swing arc, and `Z` de-ignites. An ignited blade also cuts physical tissue that moves into it without requiring swing force
- Arsenal primary actions are distinct: physical nail, pellet, bullet and saw projectiles; suction and progressive shredding; grounded sledge crushing; physical piston punches and charged uppercuts; collision-aware grenade throws; singularities, chewing rats, growth pulses, flame, ice, lightning, acid and water; and the paired baseball bat and ball. The sledge lands its authored face directly on the support plane, holds the impact, slowly lifts through a few short-lived thick blood strands, then returns to ready only after those strands snap. A full charge bounces all same-level conveyor matter to varied bounded heights; an ordinary hit bounces only its crushed body and local gore. Saw discs physically excise their complete swept cutting band instead of passing harmlessly through tissue.
- Holding LMB during a melee weapon's swing, impact, or recovery buffers the next charge. Its windup begins automatically on the first available post-recovery fixed step and is cancelled normally if LMB is released early.
- The acid lobber fires one physical ball that bursts on impact into bounded green granular acid. Acid pixels use the same gravity, pile, blob, wall, and conveyor collision path as blood, repeatedly corrode contacted tissue, and are explicitly rejected by the blood collector and basin.
- Frozen blobs suppress blink/hurt facial animation and use rigid shape matching instead of ordinary soft-body jiggle. Before their particle collision radii expand to the visible irregular ice shell, grounded bodies shift quietly away from their supporting surface with no inherited bounce; first-impact descendants retain the smaller frozen shell and collision state until their terminal shatter.
- Holding a grenade freezes the grenade at its initial LMB position while cursor motion changes a collision-aware predicted arc. Tube/conveyor/wall bounces remain visible, but prediction terminates at the first marked blob contact instead of inventing an unreadable post-impact path. Preview and live grenades use surviving particle circles rather than a stale whole-body radius; a bounded cluster-separation pass plus minimum escape speed prevents the projectile from embedding, balancing, or gluing itself to tissue. RMB cancels the throw and returns to normal rotation/movement. Grenades never cook in the hand: the fixed fuse starts on release, and the projectile collides with the same solid factory and conveyor surfaces as other physical matter.
- Equipping the sling or pike shows an alpha ghost. Left click places the valid ghost; `E` on the deployed tool picks it back up. The sling snaps north-facing to a conveyor with short, medium, or tall fork height, or rotates east/west when mounted to the left/right wall. Bringing a grabbed blob into its rubber cradle auto-loads it; grab that blob again, stretch the rendered bands, and release to launch it. Hard launches damage the fired blob, the struck blob, and wall/tube-glass contact material. The pike ghost is restricted to background wall positions above and clear of the conveyor; once placed, the pike cannot be pushed by blobs and holds punctured matter until it is grabbed free or destroyed
- Melee swings occur only from the equipped tool's left-click action; legacy cursor up/down motion no longer triggers a swing. The cleaver's charged physical arc remains relative to the player's selected base rotation and never flips its sprite based on cursor direction. Heavy cutting-edge contacts create a short contact freeze and a 235 ms Pixel Forge-authored impact/tissue burst at the exact collision point
- Left-click and hold a loose non-blob tool to reposition it directly without equipping or attacking. Releasing near the dumbwaiter socket returns the current weapon to its mount; a tool lost offscreen returns after a short delay
- Right mouse damage gestures are disabled in the playable continuous-flow factory; the butcher's cleaver is the active processing tool
- F9 / F10: deterministic horizontal / angled cut (developer regression controls)
- Start each day by grabbing the main breaker's cyan handle and pulling it downward. The lamps wake from left to right before the centered day announcement and blob flow begin. Pull the live handle all the way back upward to end the day; the lights shut down in reverse order before the day-end announcement and payout summary. Dragging the breaker housing still repositions it and saves the new placement
- Blobs are buffeted and spun by distributed air pressure as they travel right-to-left through the sealed overhead glass tube. After clearing the screen completely, they spend a short beat in unseen return plumbing before re-entering from behind the left wall on the conveyor and leaving through the matching right-wall opening
- Machinery is dormant and hidden in this continuous-flow experiment; the centered industrial dumbwaiter provides the active processing tool. It begins each unpowered day closed, starts its smooth display-rate opening as soon as the breaker latches, and closes permanently after the player takes the delivered weapon. A broken blob bond has a small chance to eject one physical reroll coin. The 32-pixel coin and matching enlarged physical body ride the conveyor and can be lost offscreen; left-drag and release it over the dumbwaiter's coin slot to arm the adjacent button, then press the button to destroy the current weapon and call the shutter open with its replacement. Only one coin may exist or be deposited at a time
- Blood and granular tissue traverse almost the entire conveyor before reaching one wide collector beside the right exit. They remain visible while traveling through its down-left pipe into the conserved glass basin
- `R`: rebuild the sample scene
- `G`: toggle gravity
- `M`: toggle the debug overlay. Its compact buttons independently show/hide FPS, blob contact circles, bonds, held-tool/projectile colliders, and detailed metrics; `ALL+`/`ALL-` change every optional layer
- `Space`: apply an upward test impulse
- `F11`, `Alt+Enter`, or the top-right button: toggle fullscreen without resetting the scene
- `Escape`: pause/resume; the pause menu exposes settings, fullscreen, quit, and a persistent gallons/liters selector for the basin readout

## Current lab implementation

- Fixed 120 Hz simulation with a hard cap of four catch-up steps per rendered frame.
- A fixed 1280×720 logical world is aspect-fitted into every window size. A 16:9 borderless fullscreen fills the selected monitor at its current resolution and refresh rate; other aspect ratios receive only the letterboxing needed to preserve the whole arena. Windowed 1280×720 renders directly, while scaled/fullscreen frames render every raster and vector layer into a normal managed bitmap before a native nearest-neighbor `StretchDIBits` presentation. This avoids both GDI+'s costly software resample and the raster-dropping behavior of drawing sprites directly into a raw DIB-backed surface. Paint requests are capped at 60 Hz, but the host pump remains independent and fine-grained so the fixed 120 Hz simulation and grab-target sampling are not batched behind render deadlines. BlobForge requests 1 ms Windows timer resolution only while its window is running and releases it on close; this prevents coarse `Task.Delay` wakeups from turning nominal 8.33 ms physics ticks into alternating back-to-back steps and 16–32 ms gaps.
- The pause menu stops simulation accumulation and cleanly releases active grabs. Its settings expose fullscreen, gravity, and the debug overlay; the always-visible top-right control provides direct fullscreen/windowed switching.
- The basin monitor reports calibrated physical volume with one decimal place rather than its internal cell count. The current authored tank uses the 64 px/m world scale, a one-meter estimated depth, and US-gallon/liter conversion from the same authoritative stored volume used by day-end sales. Fresh profiles default to gallons; the Settings selector persists the chosen unit and the matching per-unit blood rate is shown in the payout summary.
- The first progression loop is playable. Only a lineage that entered from the left conveyor doorway and then suffered real local damage adds one processed count, and it can add that count only once regardless of later fragments or repeated hits. Pulling the breaker up sells the exact stored basin volume at the current blood rate, adds a separate damaged-blob processing payment, drains the visible basin evenly as conserved blood pixels enter the trucks, and presents an itemized full-screen result. `CONTINUE TO SHOP` opens the between-day procurement screen with weapon-upgrade and weapon-unlock tabs. Each new day rolls one of the player's unlocked weapons, with a strong bias toward weapons bought during the immediately preceding shop period. During the day, physical dumbwaiter coins provide the only normal reroll path. `CONTINUE TO NEXT DAY` advances the persistent calendar, including `YEAR n • DAY n` display after day 365, and returns to the dark breaker scene.
- The environment uses a custom 4×4 indexed-color factory tilesheet generated at native 32×32 resolution. Steel, concrete, lab-glass, grate, vent, trim, and reinforced-panel variants use a sterile neutral palette that leaves the blob silhouette and vivid blood visually distinct. Static terrain is topology-cached and rebuilt only after terrain damage rather than redrawing every source tile every frame.
- The playable factory now uses a Pixel Forge-authored ceiling tube with an exact standard-blob aperture. Distributed, deterministic air gusts push and deform canonical soft bodies rather than translating or rotating them along a rigid scripted target; their irregular spin and glass impacts emerge from particle momentum and tube-wall contact. The lower glass face physically rejects carried tools and ordinary blobs. Exterior blob strikes report the same real terrain-impact point and normal speed as a wall or floor, so high-speed slingshot bodies splatter and frozen bodies shatter instead of harmlessly bouncing. Tube-owned bodies remain isolated from outside blob collisions, mouse picking, and tool damage. A body must clear the viewport completely, spend a short beat in unseen return plumbing, and only then re-enter behind the left wall onto the offscreen-spanning conveyor. Pixel Forge-authored recessed tunnel jambs match the five opened wall cells without extending a rectangular frame into the room. The former chamber, receiving tub, belt gaps, cart, worker scaffold, and manual request UI remain available only to legacy regression scenarios and are not rendered in continuous-flow play.
- The starting handheld tool remains the 72×40 Pixel Forge-authored butcher's cleaver, now presented inside the centered industrial dumbwaiter instead of a standalone rack. Its corrected blade-left/handle-right construction aligns the handle and hanging hole along the spine, with the long bevel on the opposite cutting side. Hovering a loose cleaver exposes only a compact `E` key prompt; no redundant pickup text or equipped drop popup is shown. `E` still equips/drops the tool while direct left-drag remains available. Ordinary up/down hand movement never triggers an attack: every melee swing begins with left click. Equipped carry begins blade-up, and left-click reaches full charge in 0.70 seconds with a full-charge physical shake. Its bar stays hidden for quick clicks, reveals near 25% fill, then becomes less transparent as it fills before turning amber at full charge. Release drives the authored arc relative to the player's selected base rotation, independent of cursor travel during the charge. The cutting gate measures both grip translation and angular velocity at the blade midpoint. A sufficiently charged cutting-edge hit enters a brief physical Impact pause and plays the four-frame `CleaverHeavyImpact.png` contact/tissue burst from the authoritative edge-contact point before the fast Recovery. Strong damage requires a valid LMB windup, forward edge alignment, and an unused hit token, preventing carrying or shaking from manufacturing power. Released tools inherit primarily their simulated spring-grip velocity rather than having momentum replaced by a key/mouse-up sample. Successful cuts and physical blood-pixel contacts add bounded, contact-local wet marks that dry into permanent stains and remain attached in the cleaver's rotating local frame. On the conveyor, a dropped cleaver receives one physical landing torque, then the belt carries it linearly while contact friction settles its rotation instead of continuously rolling it. Releasing within the socket capture radius begins a visible high-speed return and reattaches only after reaching the dumbwaiter.
- Continuous-flow blobs now use a Pixel Forge-directed cute palette: mint tissue, a dark teal stepped rim, large cream eyes, dark pupils, pink cheeks, and a tiny expressive mouth. The face has three authored states in one spritesheet: neutral, a deterministic occasional 120 ms blink, and a pinched-eye/frown hurt expression that overrides a blink for roughly 0.30–0.44 seconds whenever real tissue is touched by damage. The authored face follows the body's physical rotation while the live material contour continues to deform and split. Physical blood pixels coat the exact blob surface particles they strike; these marks remain attached through deformation, dry permanently, and migrate with their material if a stained component separates. Detached material remains visibly gruesome while a controlled mint/teal subset of its granular pixels preserves the source blob color instead of every fragment becoming generic red gore.
- Blob identity now includes a deterministic movement personality: a meaningful quiet population never self-hops, while playful blobs receive individually varied jumpiness, jump strength, initial delay, and recurring cadence. A free blob can trigger its hop only from real support, producing bounded physical rises rather than scripted position changes; grabbed, frozen, detached, crumbling, machine-owned, and portal-transition bodies cannot self-launch. Inside the overhead tube, the same personality timer contributes smaller air-assisted upward kicks to the existing distributed airflow, adding individual bumps and tumbles without synchronizing the population or allocating per frame.
- Once a blob has made top contact with the system conveyor, every one of its physical particles is constrained to the top run across the belt footprint. A particle that tunnels through the belt in one solver step is recovered to the top with downward velocity removed, preventing hanging bodies from becoming permanent below-belt blockers.
- All five machinery implementations remain preserved for legacy regressions, but continuous-flow play neither renders nor updates them. Passing blobs remain fully physical on the uninterrupted belt and leave through the right-wall portal.
- Captured trough particles become a capped buoyant-drop layer, bob at the basin surface, and dissolve gradually into the conserved volume instead of disappearing on contact. Interior splashes remain clipped to glass and sides. The 289×30 liquid grid retains hydrostatic pressure equalization, while low-frequency bands, a 19-column filter, and deterministic sub-cell roughness produce a natural non-sawtooth surface. At 100%, the conveyor, machinery, and physical drain remain open. Routed blood still visibly travels through the collector, pipe, and funnel into the tank but adds no stored/spendable value; its impact disturbs the full surface and alternates the displaced physical amount evenly over the left and right lips. The former persistent front-glass overflow trails are disabled pending a better presentation. Bubble travel uses stable mean depth rather than the changing slosh surface, eliminating inflow-driven jumping. Exact scalar volume includes both fractional-cell and pending suspended mass. Diego remains fully dormant, and bubbles remain absent below 35% fill.
- The earlier blood-worker/shop prototype and its scaffold remain preserved for the legacy factory regression scene, but are dormant and hidden with the machinery during the continuous-flow experiment.
- The crusher now collides as one backing plate plus five actual triangular teeth matching the artwork, allowing tissue to wrap into the gaps and visibly conform around each damaging tip. Damage radius, strength, and one-hit-per-tip behavior are unchanged. The holding chamber hatch keeps its existing physical aperture timing but renders as two outer-hinged flaps rotating downward instead of horizontally sliding panels.
- The former repeated grates and full-width trough are dormant. One Pixel Forge-authored two-pass end drain sits beside the right portal: a broad but localized collector frame is collision-aligned to a visible down-left pipe, with granular blood/tissue rendered between its back and foreground frames until the pipe mouth reaches the basin. Its four-frame state sheet provides separate open/closed back and foreground layers; the closed steel slide plate visibly seals the collector throat at capacity. Mid-belt matter cannot route or disappear, and no collector geometry interrupts the rest of the conveyor.
- Filled hex-lattice tissue rather than a hollow perimeter ring.
- Soft-compliance XPBD bonds and triangle-local area constraints create visible sag, compression, and recovery without a whole-body area constraint.
- Array/struct physics data with no per-particle controls, rigidbodies, or scene objects.
- Initial and spawned blobs use one shared archetype, keeping radius, tissue resolution, mass, bond density, and grab response identical.
- Representation modes selected by a budget scheduler rather than game-visible quality tiers.
- Weighted point grabbing transfers force through a local tissue region.
- Grab targets are shape-aware and arena-bounded. Free-space target slew is fast enough to stay synchronized with the pointer, but automatically yields under real blob contact so manipulation cannot overpower hull separation. Their bounds are frozen from the grab-time material footprint instead of recalculated from an already compressed body, preventing recursive wall/floor flattening. Horizontal wall targets now receive a larger fixed material-scaled compression allowance beyond the rigid-fit position: collision still keeps every particle inside the arena while the unblocked side continues toward the pointer and produces a substantial wall squish without permitting recursive pancake collapse.
- Throw release velocity is distributed through the full tissue mass, and airborne representation damping is deliberately low so a blob retains meaningful momentum instead of leaving most motion in the grabbed nodes and stopping immediately.
- Exposed-particle spatial hashing plus a soft center guard provides deformable blob-to-blob collision without hidden interior contact explosions.
- A final convex-hull separation constraint matches the visible blob skin and prevents lattice particles from slipping through surface gaps or becoming intertwined under repeated hard jamming. When either body is deliberately held, this guard now permits a controlled 78%-of-particle-spacing shell compression band before intervening, so particle and area constraints can visibly mush two blobs together without allowing deep interlock.
- After terrain and blob resolution, a hard whole-body arena projection rejects every active tissue particle and removes only velocity directed farther into the wall. Blobs cannot remain embedded or be solver-pushed back through the outer boundary.
- Contact projection preserves velocity, and pressure-specific strain damping prevents a grabbed blob from releasing another blob like a compressed spring. Free throws retain their ordinary dynamics.
- Grabbed pressure separates gravity-derived motion from contact oscillation: falling and tangential sliding remain active, while repeated pressure-axis velocity is dissipated. An unheld blob therefore cannot stick to the side of a held blob or hover because gravity was accidentally damped.
- Blob-on-blob contacts provide stable support, allowing stacks and piles to enter deterministic sleep.
- Blob separation is environment-aware without deadening contact: a floor-supported body retains 35% of its ordinary inverse mass for a correction aimed farther into the floor, allowing it to move and deform under another blob while the terrain solver independently prevents clipping. Local particle contacts provide the visible soft-body pressure. Emergency hull separation has a bounded per-iteration translation budget, and a held body's grab target follows only 20% of that whole-body safety correction; combined with the deeper manipulation-only soft overlap band, the pointer can keep applying playful pressure before the anti-intertwining backstop engages. Pressure damping is capped at once per body per solver contact pass instead of being multiplied by every colliding particle pair. A passive blob under deliberate held pressure temporarily uses softer bonds, then regains its ordinary stiffness after a 40 ms contact tail so stored deformation produces a controlled separation recoil instead of being erased or becoming an explosive launch. Terrain is resolved again after the final pass, and a particle whose center is forced inside a solid chooses a genuinely exposed face using its previous position and movement rather than escaping through an internal tile seam or the underside of the arena. Sustained held pressure is regressed for 480 steps with zero permitted floor penetration and at least 18% passive-body deformation; a wall-backed two-blob test additionally requires visible deformation and recoil after release, while the combined wall/two-blob case is stressed for 600 steps without runaway stretching.
- Contact diagnostics are independent from vertical support. Terrain, conveyor, arena-wall, and blob contacts set a short-lived all-direction contact flag rendered yellow in lattice debug mode; only upward support contributes to grounded viscosity, sleep, and gravity behavior. Side-wall contacts therefore display correctly without pretending to be a floor.
- Stable support plus a quiet-time threshold enters deterministic sleep. Sleeping blobs have exactly zero integration drift until a real event wakes them.
- Material cells provide broad-phase collision, impact damage, and event-based destruction.
- Tissue bonds carry health. Damage queues an event-driven connected-component pass and removes single-bond bridges so interior particles cannot remain dangling like pendulums.
- Isolated point damage inside cohesive tissue is projected to the nearest real outer surface, producing a visible local bite instead of hidden core bleeding. Stationary cutting is rate-limited separately from moving cuts so a click cannot chew through several tissue layers in a few frames.
- RMB gestures use separate damage tools. A press released within a 7 px movement threshold applies one wide localized bite on release. Crossing the threshold switches to a thin deferred slice: the path is rendered as a cyan preview without changing topology, then commits when it exits through another blob edge. Releasing early commits exactly the partial path accumulated so far. The committed polyline evaluates every bond once regardless of mouse polling density and merges aligned samples into stable seam runs. Stationary slices add neither path nor damage.
- Rendering and blob collision consume one authoritative material contour. Its cohesive component, directed boundary loop, wound flags, and segment groups are built once per topology revision and cached; ordinary render/collision queries perform only linear particle sampling and cached-array relaxation. Loop identity comes from topology rather than changing geometric area, and a required cohesive-hull fallback remains latched until the next real damage event instead of alternating frame by frame. The headless contour benchmark covers 20 damaged bodies across 600 frames and verifies exactly one topology build per body.
- Damaged cut-face fitting, smoothing, and final curve construction enforce contact containment: straight wound seams are placed outside their supporting boundary nodes, smoothing cannot outrun the skin allowance, and a bounded local shell repair covers any remaining physical center. Repeated point bites can leave multiple intact area islands touching at one lattice node; whenever the largest edge-connected island does not include every physical particle, the authoritative contour now falls back to the complete physical hull for surviving blobs as well as detached chunks. The rendered damaged curve is validated against those same centers and uses the validated rounded polygonal shell only for the exceptional concavity that would otherwise bow inward. Collision/contact debug nodes therefore cannot sit beyond the visible material.
- Slice geometry is retained through topology splitting without collapsing physical lattice rows onto the cutter. Only boundary edges permanently associated with the wound may bind to a seam; arbitrary nearby perimeter vertices can no longer snap on and off a proximity threshold. Each seam run is fitted as one straight line and shifted toward its child until every projected vertex remains within the supported material skin, producing matching clean faces without inventing an unsupported tail.
- Recorded cutter segments live in each component's material-local frame rather than screen/world space. A best-fit rigid transform estimated from current versus reference tissue particles carries those segments through translation and rotation; subsequent topology splits clip every segment to that child's own cohesive wound span before rebasing it into the child frame. Stable edge bindings keep classification fixed as the body deforms.
- Topology stabilization severs damaged nodes with no intact cell and distance bonds that belong to no intact material edge. These invisible remnants enter the existing loose-fragment/pixel path instead of remaining attached as collision-only geometry.
- Coherent detached chunks participate in the same contour-derived convex-hull separation pass as full blobs, preventing the parent and chunk from overlapping, hooking, or remaining interlocked along a freshly separated surface.
- Damaged rendering rejects non-finite and near-zero-area outlines before they reach GDI+, falling back to individual tissue fragments as erosion completes. Intact blobs use a mint body and dark-teal pixel-stepped contour directed by the editable `blobforge_cute_blob_style` reference, with a Pixel Forge-authored face rotated over the live material center. Contour samples remain integer-aligned rectangle batches, so the silhouette keeps hard pixel edges while following real deformation, cuts, and detached geometry. A grounded multi-segment cut is rendered for 120 regression frames to guard against WinForms red-X paint failures.
- Granulated tissue is now predominantly solid red/dark-crimson material pixels, with only a restrained one-in-five structural fleck retaining the near-black core and red rim. Blood remains brighter solid crimson, while coherent detached chunks use a visible burgundy body and bright pixel edge; destruction therefore stays related to the source blob without turning the entire scene into identical black-centered marks.
- Cell-less components created by a point bite begin budgeted granular conversion immediately instead of surviving as barely visible ghost mini-bodies. A deterministic 120-hit repeated-bite regression checks both the authoritative shell and final rendered path around every surviving physical center. Debug lattice bonds are batched, particle brushes are reused, loose material pixels are grouped into five reusable raster paths instead of hundreds of individual GDI calls, and the metrics panel refreshes at 5 Hz. Blob contours reuse healthy surface-index and active-hull scratch buffers instead of rebuilding LINQ/sort/list intermediates; the granular simulation stress run now allocates 4034.3 KiB across 360 steps instead of 16063.3 KiB and measures 1.666 ms/step instead of 1.947 ms/step. The `--render-benchmark` stress scene covers sixteen live damaged bodies and 110 stains at 1309×920; the verified measurement is 8.21 ms/frame normally and 12.49 ms/frame with full lattice diagnostics. The station-specific benchmark advances the real 120 Hz simulation, a 35%-filled cellular basin, and every powered machine state at 1280×720; the verified run measured 7.28–8.98 ms/frame versus the 9.50–10.85 ms baseline, with a hard 13.5 ms regression ceiling. The combined `--factory-stress-benchmark` adds a compressed sixteen-blob pile, nine damaged bodies, roughly 1,500 live blood/tissue pixels, and persistent belt/terrain stains while advancing two 120 Hz ticks per rendered frame. The measured total fell from 24.10 to 18.49 ms/frame and managed allocation from 188.6 to 80.1 KiB/frame; its 25 ms process-CPU/128 KiB simulation/32 KiB render budgets now fail the command on a regression while wall time remains visible for diagnosing external contention. Static machinery art and basin structure are baked into the cached factory layer, the foreground tube strip and continuous conveyor base are cached draws, conveyor/blob paths and basin drawing resources are reused, display-sized sprite exports are resampled only once, and ambient plus occluded lighting share one composited overlay instead of two full-screen blends. Machinery loops use independent asynchronous media players and idempotent desired-state transitions instead of synchronous legacy MCI commands. The former unsupported loop command cost roughly 16 ms on every 120 Hz fixed update; `--audio-loop-benchmark` now measures 0.006 ms/update in the worst cue, and the debug panel exposes both aggregate fixed-update and audio-state time so another hidden stall cannot masquerade as rendering cost.
- The largest coherent component remains the blob. Each detached component keeps its exact deformed cut-time geometry: intact bonds and local areas are rebased at separation, and the authoritative contour preserves the portion of the visible material that was actually removed rather than replacing it with a generic shape.
- Detached chunks are ungrabbable physical soft bodies that inherit bounded momentum, collide with blobs and terrain, and use the budgeted three-iteration shape-proxy tier while at least four coherent nodes remain—including during the crumble wave. A weak velocity-preserving cluster recovery keeps the surviving material near its post-cut pose without suppressing translation, rotation, or contact deformation. Only the final one-to-three-node residue becomes a loose fragment. Every meaningful terrain or unrelated-body contact adds an independent timed erosion origin; sleeping against a same-lineage cut partner is not a landing and cannot start erosion. During erosion the remaining hard-sand material uses a stable physical-particle hull, preventing partially converted lattice triangles from rendering as crowns. A node that loses its final intact material cell is released from solving, collision, and debug rendering immediately, while its remaining mass stays queued for capped pixel emission; cell-less constraint chains therefore cannot hang outside the visible chunk.
- Fresh detached chunks retain the complete physical cut-time hull when more than one intact cell island remains in the same chunk, so the renderer cannot shrink to only the largest island while valid contact nodes survive. One- and two-node scraps also remain visible physical debris until a real impact instead of granulating on creation. All simultaneously crumbling chunks share one global three-pixel-per-step conversion budget.
- Blood pixels paint the exact terrain contact coordinate rather than tinting an entire tile. Before storage, every flat stain and procedural drip is rebound to the exposed solid face directly behind its actual world-space coordinate; samples over air are rejected, neighboring solid samples transfer ownership to that surface, and marks beside an unsupported tile edge are inset only far enough to keep their rendered pixels on material. Diagonal seam/corner impacts are canonicalized to the nearest genuinely exposed axis-aligned face before storage, preventing a one-frame impact particle from masquerading as wall paint and then disappearing. Rendering is additionally intersected with one topology-cached region made from the live solid cells, so width noise, segmented-drip wandering, and beads cannot draw even one persistent pixel into an air gap; the region rebuilds only when terrain solidity changes. Each local pool now keeps one stable primary runoff lane: that lane captures the surrounding pool pressure and can visibly cross two or more connected cells, while satellite drips retain their smaller individual scale and irregular variation instead of becoming a uniform curtain. Persistent length is shared by rendering and physical tip emission and can reach 120 px when supported material continues below. The production-density check yields seven multi-cell leaders among twenty-one runoff marks, and the rendered regression requires visible staining in the third connected cell. Side-wall impacts use wall-normal speed rather than the generic total-speed gate: the preselected splatter minority bursts at moderate horizontal impact, exceptionally hard ordinary droplets have a small additional burst chance, and the majority continue to bounce while leaving localized contact stains. Wall pigment uses a narrower merge radius and stops merging into saturated marks, so repeated sprays visibly add coverage; flat wall marks also keep their vertical tangent extent instead of sharing the five-pixel floor-height cap. Wet wall deposits receive protected stain-layer priority so a saturated floor cannot evict them before runoff forms, real particle-sized wall marks mature into trails sooner, and dry wall pigment fades more slowly than exposed floor paint. A rendered symmetry regression verifies comparable left/right coverage and visible additions after a second burst; a separate capacity-stress regression keeps feeding the floor while requiring persistent stains and active runoff on both walls. Vertical-face deposits have a separate mass/cadence accumulator that creates downward wall-attached trails and eventually releases ordinary falling pixels from the same computed wet tip. A capped active surface-mark layer retains impact position and surface normal, merges only overlapping wet deposits, renders localized wet/dry marks, and never scans untouched world cells.
- Paint deposition per blood contact is intentionally restrained, runoff creation is globally throttled, and stain counts use spatial coalescing thresholds (640 terrain marks and 192 conveyor marks) instead of eviction limits. Stains dry from bright wet red to darker pigment but never auto-delete; only loss of the supporting surface, or a future explicit cleaning mechanic, may remove them. Once a coalescing threshold is reached, later contacts merge into an existing mark on the same surface face/loop region while genuinely new terrain faces remain paintable. Recent-cell merging avoids full-list searches during active bleeding. Localized marks render as non-antialiased material pixels and tapered, laterally wandering segmented drips with a fixed brush palette.
- Surface marks carry deterministic size/spacing variation so pooled blood does not form a uniform fringe. Flat conveyor pigment is stored as a coordinate on the belt material's closed capsule loop: signed belt speed advances those stains across the top run, around the powered end roller, along the return run, and around the opposite roller. Reversing the conveyor reverses flat pigment circulation. A sufficiently wet top-run pool can establish one short-lived drip point anchored to the stationary conveyor frame at the exact spill location. That point emits a bounded cadence of falling transient blood droplets but creates no persistent vertical trail, does not follow the tread, and cannot seed a row of emitters along the belt. Dense underside deposits can also shed occasional falling pixels, with at most six transient conveyor drops visible at once. The renderer uses the same loop for its continuous belt outline, curved end treads, return treads, and roller rotation.
- Environment drips are selected from pooled wet-mark density in surface/world coordinates rather than tile seams. Runoff is deliberately irregular rather than lane-balanced: every pool first establishes a primary trail, then high mass only gives a probability of adding one or two asymmetrically offset satellites. Transfer amount, growth target, radius ceiling, initial phase, length, and width are independently seeded, so one heavy primary can coexist with short thin side drips instead of converging to uniform bars. Wet active tips accumulate flow at different rates and occasionally emit a capped physical blood pixel from their rendered tip; that pixel enters the ordinary granular simulation, falls with varied size/speed, collides, and can repaint a lower surface. Flat pigment is quantized to small 2 px dark-red tiles and inset into the contacted surface plane from its collision normal; only the 2.5D drip layer extends downward from the exact pooled surface coordinate.
- Wound blood is a controlled mixture: roughly 70% remains ordinary simulated bouncing/sliding blood pixels, while 30% becomes impact-splatter pixels. The splatter threshold is 45 px/s so normal wound impacts can visibly trigger it without converting slow resting blood. Most droplets use a deep arterial crimson while a smaller deterministic subset uses a vivid fresh-red highlight; wet stain highlights share the vivid red rather than the former pink cast. A splatter impact consumes its particle into nine-to-fourteen randomized paint hits across a speed-scaled 18–36 px tangent span. The center receives denser pigment while supported outer hits form the broad splash silhouette; samples crossing a real gap are discarded rather than floating, and the result remains inside the same capped stain layer.
- Existing slow blood and tissue pixels have a very small density-weighted chance to move into the 2.5D foreground at their current world X; the chance rises sharply as their local 32×24 px neighborhood piles up. The 10 Hz transition budget scales from eight to thirty-two existing pixels under severe pile pressure, while a 256-entry hard cap prevents a new performance spike. A transition removes that exact source from the physical pile and renders one particle-sized square—never a cloned bead trail—while retaining blood versus tissue kind, radius, and tissue palette. Foreground blood/tissue falling over the open basin is consumed into its authoritative stored volume using the same per-pixel yield rules. At capacity, that same foreground pixel returns to the ordinary physical granular tier at an alternating lip with varied outward/upward velocity, so it ejects, collides, and bounces before any later supported-pile selection can move it into 2.5D again. Other falls leave through the bottom of the room. Fast impacts, acid, and matter already traveling inside a drain never enter this tier.
- Conveyors are editable first-class solid surfaces. Their top contacts converge soft tissue toward configurable belt velocity without adding vertical launch energy; sides remain blocking, granular material rides the belt, and conveyor-local blood marks move with a repositioned belt and share the localized wet/dry runoff behavior. A visible `SPAWN CONVEYOR` button or `C` creates and selects a belt. Left-dragging its body moves it, the selected right/lower handles resize length/height, the mouse wheel changes speed, and double-click reverses direction. `Tab`, arrow keys, `Shift+arrow keys`, `+`/`-`, and `V` remain keyboard equivalents. The renderer shows a continuous top/return tread loop and mechanically matching rotating end/support rollers rather than static decorative dots.
- Bond breaks create persistent wound emitters. Severe wounds bleed faster and longer; flow decays continuously until the wound clots instead of producing one unlimited spray.
- Blood launch speed, active wounds, topology jobs, per-step blood/tissue creation, and total granular particles are capped. Exterior granular-to-blob contact uses the surface-particle spatial hash. A second body-AABB hash catches pixels that crossed a surface gap; only a nearby blood pixel found inside the authoritative visible contour triggers a nearest-edge projection. That contour is built lazily at most once per contacted body per granular step, preventing trapped interior blood without scanning every blob or rebuilding a contour for every pixel.
- Blood and detached tissue pixels fall, bounce, slide, collide one-way with heavier blob tissue, and settle against neighboring material pixels without kicking or reshaping their own source chunk.
- Per-iteration correction limits protect crushed or temporarily inverted tissue cells from solver explosions.
- The `M` debug overlay defaults to lightweight FPS, blob-contact, and tool/projectile-collider inspection. Compact `ALL+`, `ALL-`, `FPS`, `BLOBS`, `BONDS`, `TOOLS`, and `METRICS` buttons change those layers independently. The expensive detailed text and display panels are opt-in, so ordinary collider testing does not build their strings, LINQ totals, or large bitmap overlays. When enabled, metrics report paint/FPS timing, allocation, contact/topology data, display geometry, cache rebuilds, and presentation state; GPU-only fields remain explicitly not applicable to the CPU-side WinForms/GDI+ renderer.

## Production direction

The filled bonded-particle model, local XPBD constraints, damage events, topology splitting, representation scheduler, and regression harness are now implemented. The GDI+ convex-hull renderer is still disposable; it will be replaced by SDL3 GPU metaball rendering so cuts, holes, and irregular components are represented directly from the tissue field.

The next slices are GPU material-field rendering, machinery contacts, blob-to-blob spatial-hash contacts, loose fragments/fluid, and population representation transitions. See the architecture document for the decision matrix and milestone exit tests.
